## 1. Who the page is for — from the verified knowledge, not from taste

The formal ICP ranking is still open (`Sales-Machine` `U-005`, Interview #1
not held). Everything below rests on the graded cards in
`Sales-Machine/knowledge/` — `segments/buyers.yaml`, `segments/sales-motion.yaml`,
`claims/public-claims.yaml`, `answers/answer-bank.yaml` — all `doc_confirmed`
from ספר העבודה 2026-08-31, with Tom's written confirmations where noted.

**Two people read this page, and they want different things.**

| | The decider — owner / manager | The influencer — bar manager / barista |
|---|---|---|
| Cares about | profit, risk, headache | taste, prep time, variety |
| Does not care about | tea varieties, leaf origin, brand story | — |
| What closes | ₪ per cup vs. ₪ on the menu · no machine · no fridge space · no big stock | recipes · variety · ten-second prep · training |
| Tell-tale questions | price first · minimum · "how much do I make" | recipe · what fits my menu · equipment |
| Can they say yes? | signs | cannot sign, can kill it |

**Four venues, four different first products** (ספר העבודה §06):

| Venue | Entry product | The argument |
|---|---|---|
| Café | one tea concentrate | bottle + water + ice, zero equipment; a category between the coffee and the fridge that adds to the menu instead of competing with it |
| Restaurant | the four caffeine-free concentrates | an afternoon offer, and something for the diner who is not drinking; allergens are the caution |
| Bar | chai (NAMASTEA) | eleven drinks from what is already behind the bar — tonic, espresso, orange juice, milk |
| Specialty coffee | matcha | frother and milk are already there; lead with ₪2.13 a serve, never ₪590 a tin; the ₪170 kit is the entry offer |
| Hotel / catering / office | — | named as a target, no script, `U-015` — the page should not pretend to know |

**How GT actually sells** (`sales-motion.yaml`): the goal is a first order, not a
meeting; the real objection is inertia, not price; nothing is sent before it is
asked for; what is sent is *three recipes from the category that brought the
lead*, never the 48; training after the order is part of the sale, because "my
staff won't learn" is the objection that kills reorders. Tom's line today makes
that the positioning, not a footnote: GT sells the drinks *selling*, not the
bottle.

**What GT may claim in public** (`public-claims.yaml`, all approved): ₪3.25 → ₪20
on a cup; less profitable than coffee, far more than a can; no preservatives,
colours or flavourings; made in Holon; kosher Badatz Beit Yosef (Tom, in
writing); shelf life a year closed without refrigeration, three months open
(Tom, in writing); ~700 customers, 8 years (Tom, in writing); six customers may
be named — R2M, Kisu Group, Nono Mimi, Vivino, Elita Ofek, Babka; nationwide
delivery via Ice Dream on fixed days; minimum order ₪800 + VAT.

### What the page does with that today

The page is built for the **influencer**: ten collections, 48 costed recipes, a
mixer, a bar-tools grid, a step-by-step modal — genuinely stronger than anything
in the benchmark (nobody else shows cost, price and margin per cup). The
**decider** gets the two numbers, the price list and six FAQ answers. What the
decider does not get, and the sales motion says they ask first: **minimum order,
delivery days, shelf life, kashrut, and what happens after they order.** Nothing
on the page sorts the visitor by venue — the one question GT asks every lead
before saying anything else. The hero is a carousel of collections, so a
stranger's first second is "Iced Tea · 7 drinks · recommended ₪20", not "what is
this and is it for me". The primary call to action, "רוצים להתחיל", promises
nothing concrete; the tasting the sales motion leads with is one option inside a
dropdown at the bottom of the form. And an existing customer who lands on the
new homepage — the 87–98 % who reorder — finds no door to order or log in.

## 2. What the leading sites do — seven patterns, with the evidence

**P1 · The hero names the customer and the outcome, in one line.**
Jade Leaf: "PREMIUM MATCHA. CAFÉ READY." Prana Chai: "Serve chai that is the
same standard as speciality coffee", with the trust strip "Used by 4,000+ venues
in 57 countries. Fast setup, staff training included." Oatly: three tiles,
"Cafes & Restaurants / Schools & Cafeterias / Offices". Cuzen: "Café and
restaurant / Hotel and Spa / Office". Takumi (IL): "מאצ׳ה איכותית לעסקים
בישראל", then a business-type menu (בתי קפה · עגלות קפה · מסעדות ומלונות ·
קונדיטוריות · חנויות). The 1883 and Boiron heroes are the counter-example —
beautiful and silent about who it is for.

**P2 · The primary button is the lowest-commitment step; buying is the second door.**
Jade Leaf: "REQUEST FREE SAMPLES" first, "PLACE AN ORDER" second. Prana:
"Enquire Now" beside "Order Now" for existing stockists. Rishi, Teapigs,
Alchemy, Dona: an enquiry/application beside a login. Monin UK: a "Request a
Sample?" checkbox inside the form. Takumi: "קבלת מחירון ודוגמה" repeated in hero,
mid-page and footer.

**P3 · One routing question at the top of the form, then a short form.**
Sweetbird/Beyond the Bean: "Type of business" (10 options) — required, first.
Boiron: "Are you a: Distributor / User", then Bar, Hotel, Catering… Rishi:
business type (10), product-line interest, *preferred method of contact*
(email / phone / text). Minor Figures: type of business + preferred distributor.
Monin Hub: number of stores. The 13-field forms (1883, Boiron) are the
counter-example nobody should copy. Best forms: 5–8 fields.

**P4 · What happens next is written down, with a clock and a human.**
Rishi: "someone from our team will be in touch in about 48 hours." Monin Hub:
"within 48 hours." Oatly: no form at all — a named account manager with direct
mobile and e-mail. Shott: six regional managers with names, mobiles, e-mails.
Alchemy: three named reps with mobiles. Cuzen's thank-you explains *why* there
may be a follow-up question. GT's form already says "עונים תוך יום עסקים אחד" —
the clock exists; the human does not.

**P5 · The operator's objections are answered on the page, not in the call.**
Teapigs puts "we don't have a MOQ" in the hero sentence. Alchemy's FAQ above the
form: no minimum, flat shipping, "24-month shelf life, no refrigeration after
opening", who qualifies. Rishi: public wholesale prices and the free-shipping
threshold. Jade Leaf: public prices, free expedited shipping over $29. Shott:
kosher / halal / vegan flags on every product. Takumi: samples, business price
list, staff training, ongoing supply, a 13-question FAQ.

**P6 · The service is listed right above the form — "what you get beyond the bottle".**
Dona: "EDUCATION & SUPPORT — tea cuppings for staff training… brewing guides,
custom specialty drink recipes", directly above "Work with us". DaVinci: three
promises (merchandising material · sales & marketing support · one-on-one
training) immediately followed by "Get In Touch With Our Team". Torani names
free menu development, menu-board and barista-guide design. Takumi: a 5-step
process — intake → pricing → recipe build → staff training → supply & tracking
— and "לא רק שקית מאצ׳ה". This is Tom's sentence, already on the best sites.

**P7 · Proof is a number, named venues, and one operator's voice.**
Prana: 4,000+ venues, 57 countries, four café logos, and a manager's quote.
Alchemy: "10,000 cafe owners… 25 years", BRCGS. Teapigs: 180 Great Taste
Awards. Sweetbird: 50 countries, OTIF 97 %. GT has the logos band and nothing
else in numbers — while "8 years" and "~700 customers" are approved and unused.

Also seen, and **not** for GT: scarcity framing ("partnerships are limited" —
Matchaful; "not accepting applications" — Ippodo US); prices behind a login
(Teapigs, Boiron, Dona — GT's open price list is a differentiator and Tom chose
to keep it for now); training academies with certificates (Monin, DaVinci, 1883)
— GT trains in the venue, which is the better promise; recipe generators and
"my shelf" gadgets (Sweetbird). Worth knowing: not one of the eighteen global
sites uses WhatsApp or a sticky mobile button; the Israeli leader (Takumi) uses
both, and GT's whole sales motion runs on WhatsApp.

## 4. The hero — three directions for the decision with Tom

Every direction keeps: one still photograph instead of the ten-slide carousel
(the carousel's copy already exists as "עשר קולקציות" below); the sr-only H1
becomes the visible one; the eyebrow, headline, sub-line, trust line and two
buttons; the trust line built only from approved claims. The hero photographs
that exist are the ten collection slides — a photograph that says "this is a
drink on a café table, made from that bottle" is Tom's to choose or shoot.

| | A · the category (Tom's phrase) | B · the number first | C · the service first (Tom's sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eyebrow | GT Everyday · חולון | GT Everyday · תמציות לבתי קפה | GT Everyday |
| Headline | **עידן חדש של משקאות — בבית הקפה שלכם.** | **48 משקאות. בקבוק אחד. מ־₪2.85 לכוס.** | **לא רק מספקים משקאות. עוזרים לכם למכור אותם.** |
| Sub-line | תמציות תה טבעיות, מאצ׳ה ופירה פירות מהמפעל שלנו — עם המתכונים המתומחרים, ההדרכה והאספקה שהופכים אותם למשקאות שנמכרים. | תמציות תה, מאצ׳ה ופירה פירות לבתי קפה ומסעדות — ואיתם המתכון המתומחר, הדרכת הצוות והאספקה הקבועה. | תמציות תה, מאצ׳ה ופירה פירות מהמפעל בחולון, עם מתכון מתומחר לכל משקה, הדרכה לצוות ואספקה קבועה. |
| Trust line | 8 שנים · כ־700 בתי קפה ומסעדות · אספקה לכל הארץ | same | same |
| Buttons | לתיאום טעימה · למחירון ולמתכונים | same | same |
| Image | one glass on a café table, the bottle beside it | the bottle and four glasses in a row | a barista pouring 50 ml, a printed menu card in frame |
| Risk | "עידן חדש" says *new*, not *what*; the sub-line carries the what | numbers first can read as a price sheet; strongest for the decider | strongest positioning; a stranger needs the sub-line to learn *what* |

Recommendation: **A's eyebrow-headline pairing with C's promise as the
sub-line** — Tom's phrase as the headline, the "what" and the "we help you sell
it" in the sub-line, the approved numbers as the trust line, the tasting as the
button. Tom decides the words; nothing is built before that.
